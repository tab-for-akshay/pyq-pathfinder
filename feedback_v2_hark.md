[Summary card]
2020-8c: Government Steps on Procurement and Marketing Constraints

15 marks, about 200 words

Intro: Farmers get only a small share of what consumers pay because of fragmented mandis, middlemen, thin procurement outside a few states, and poor storage. The government has responded on price, market access, infrastructure and institutions.

Key steps, matched to constraints

Price uncertainty: MSP for 22 mandated crops plus sugarcane FRP, fixed at least 1.5 times A2+FL cost since 2018-19. PM-AASHA (2018) adds price support (PSS), price-deficiency payments (PDPS) and private procurement (PPSS) for pulses and oilseeds.
Narrow procurement: decentralised procurement (DCP) by states, with procurement through FCI, NAFED, CCI and state agencies. Pulse buffers come under the Price Stabilisation Fund.
Fragmented, opaque mandis: Model APMC Act 2003 and Model APLM Act 2017, private markets and direct purchase, and e-NAM (2016) for online, inter-mandi trading across about 1,400 mandis.
Distance to markets: upgrading 22,000 rural haats into Gramin Agricultural Markets (GrAMs), and Kisan Rail and Krishi Udan for perishables.
Weak bargaining power: 10,000 FPOs (2020), cooperatives (PACS computerisation) and the Model Contract Farming Act 2018.
Storage and post-harvest gaps: the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (Rs 1 lakh crore), the world's largest grain storage plan in the cooperative sector (2023), and WDRA electronic warehouse receipts (e-NWR) for loans against stored produce.
Perishables and price swings: Operation Greens (TOP, extended to TOTAL), PM Kisan SAMPADA, cold chains and mega food parks.
Information gaps: Agmarknet price data, Kisan Call Centres, and market-intelligence cells at SAUs and ICAR-NIAP.
Trade: Agriculture Export Policy 2018 with cluster-based exports through APEDA.

Limits: MSP benefits stay concentrated in a few crops and states. e-NAM inter-state trade is still low, and the 2020 farm laws were repealed in 2021. Reform needs state buy-in.

Conclusion: the way forward is FPO-led aggregation, e-NAM integration with grading, and crop-neutral price support that rewards diversification.

Diagram: a value chain, Farmer → FPO/GrAM → APMC/e-NAM/private market → procurement or storage (FCI, WDRA warehouse) → processor, consumer or export. Label the scheme at each arrow.

Sources: CACP price policy reports (MSP methodology); Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare scheme guidelines (PM-AASHA, AIF, 10,000 FPOs, e-NAM); Model APLM Act 2017; Economic Survey chapters on agricultural marketing.
